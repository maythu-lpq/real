SN2 Mechanism

Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution • Concerted Backside Attack • Walden Inversion

1. Overview & Kinetics

The SN2 (Substitution, Nucleophilic, Bimolecular) reaction is a single-step, concerted displacement mechanism. The incoming nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon from the rear (180° away from the leaving group), simultaneously pushing out the leaving group through a single trigonal bipyramidal transition state.

Second-Order Kinetics

Rate = k[Substrate][Nu−] — First-order in substrate and first-order in nucleophile. Doubling either reactant doubles the rate; doubling both quadruples the rate.

2. Concerted Backside Attack & Walden Inversion

The nucleophile must donate electron density into the σ* (antibonding) orbital of the C–LG bond. This orbital geometry strictly mandates a 180° collinear backside attack.

3. Stereochemistry: 100% Inversion (Walden Inversion)

Because the nucleophile enters exclusively from the back face, the three non-reacting substituents flip over like an umbrella inverting in a strong gust of wind.

Stereospecificity

If the starting material has an (R) stereocenter, the product configuration inverts to (S) (assuming CIP priority order is conserved). Optical purity is fully retained (no racemization).

4. Controlling Factors

1. Substrate (Steric Hindrance is King)

Reactivity order: CH3 > 1° > 2° >> 3° (effectively zero). Tertiary substrates are completely blocked from backside attack. Neopentyl is exceptionally sluggish.

2. Nucleophile Strength & Polarizability

Rate depends directly on [Nu]. Strong, highly polarizable nucleophiles (I−, RS−, CN−, N3−) accelerate SN2.

3. Leaving Group

Departure occurs simultaneously with attack: I− > Br− > Cl− ≈ OTs− >> F−. OH− is too poor to leave unactivated.

4. Solvent (Polar Aprotic)

Polar aprotic solvents (DMSO, DMF, Acetone, MeCN) lack H-bonding. They dissolve counter-cations while leaving the nucleophilic anion "naked" and hyper-reactive.

5. Cross-Mechanism Relationships
