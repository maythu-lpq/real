E2 Mechanism

Bimolecular Elimination • Concerted Transition State • Anti-Periplanar Geometry

1. Overview & Kinetics

The E2 (Elimination, Bimolecular) reaction is a single-step, concerted elimination. A strong base removes a proton from the β-carbon while the leaving group simultaneously departs from the α-carbon, directly forming a carbon–carbon π bond with no intermediate carbocation.

Second-Order Kinetics

Rate = k[Substrate][Base] — Bimolecular rate law. Doubling either the substrate or base concentration doubles the overall rate. Increasing base concentration strongly promotes E2 over unimolecular pathways (E1/SN1).

2. Concerted Mechanism & The Anti-Periplanar Rule

For maximum orbital overlap between the breaking Cβ–H σ bond and the developing π system, the β-hydrogen and the leaving group must lie in the same plane with a dihedral angle of 180° (anti-periplanar).

3. Regioselectivity: Zaitsev vs. Hofmann Elimination

When a substrate has non-equivalent β-hydrogens, the identity of the base dictates which alkene isomer predominates:

Base Steric Bulk Representative Bases Dominant Product Governing Principle
Small / Unhindered OH−, NaOEt, NaOMe Zaitsev Product (more substituted alkene) Thermodynamic stability of alkene (hyperconjugation) controls lower TS energy.
Bulky / Hindered KOtBu, LDA, DBU Hofmann Product (less substituted alkene) Steric crowding prevents base from accessing internal β-H; less hindered terminal β-H is deprotonated.

4. Controlling Factors & SN2 vs. E2 Competition

1. Substrate Class

Reactivity: 3° > 2° > 1°. Bulkier substrates strongly suppress SN2 backside attack, directing strong bases exclusively to E2.

2. Base Nature

Requires a strong base (pKa of conjugate acid > 14). Bulky bases (KOtBu) completely shut down SN2 even on primary substrates.

3. Temperature

High temperature (Δ) strongly favors E2 over SN2 because elimination creates three particles from two (ΔS > 0).

4. Leaving Group & Geometry

Good leaving groups (I−, Br−, OTs−) are required. In rings, the reaction cannot occur unless H and LG can achieve a diaxial conformation.

5. Cross-Mechanism Relationships

Explore connections to other mechanisms:

  • SN2 Mechanism — Main competitor with strong reagents. Small nucleophiles, 1° substrates, and lower temperatures favor SN2.
  • E1 Mechanism — Unimolecular elimination with weak bases via carbocations. Non-stereospecific and prone to rearrangements.
  • SN1 Mechanism — Stepwise substitution via carbocation with weak nucleophiles.
