SN1
Substitution
Unimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution

A stepwise mechanism initiated by heterolytic cleavage of the C–LG bond, generating a planar carbocation intermediate followed by nucleophilic capture from either face.

  • Kinetics Rate = k[R–LG]
  • Steps 2 (Ionization & Capture)
  • Intermediate Carbocation (sp2)
  • Stereochemistry Racemization (± excess inv.)
  • Best Substrate 3° >> 2° (never 1° or CH3)

SN2
Substitution
Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution

A concerted, single-step backside attack where bond formation and bond cleavage occur simultaneously via a trigonal bipyramidal pentacoordinate transition state.

  • Kinetics Rate = k[R–LG][Nu−]
  • Steps 1 (Concerted)
  • Intermediate None (1 TS‡)
  • Stereochemistry 100% Inversion (Walden)
  • Best Substrate CH3 > 1° > 2° (never 3°)

E1
Elimination
Unimolecular Elimination

A stepwise elimination sharing the carbocation intermediate with SN1. A weak base abstracts a β-proton in the fast second step, generating the most stable Zaitsev alkene.

  • Kinetics Rate = k[R–LG]
  • Steps 2 (Ionization & Deprotonation)
  • Intermediate Carbocation (sp2)
  • Regiochemistry Zaitsev (more substituted)
  • Competitor Competes with SN1 (heat favors E1)

E2
Elimination
Bimolecular Elimination

A concerted, single-step reaction in which a strong base abstracts a β-proton simultaneously as the leaving group departs, demanding a strict anti-periplanar 180° geometry.

  • Kinetics Rate = k[R–LG][Base]
  • Steps 1 (Concerted)
  • Intermediate None (1 TS‡)
  • Stereochemistry Anti-periplanar (180° diaxial)
  • Regiochemistry Zaitsev (small base) / Hofmann (bulky)

Reaction Prediction Decision Guide

Follow the structural logic of the substrate and reagents to identify the dominant reaction pathway.

1. Methyl Substrate (CH3–LG)

Backside attack is completely unhindered. No β-hydrogen exists for elimination, and methyl carbocations cannot form.

Outcome: SN2 exclusively

2. Primary Substrate (1°, RCH2–LG)

Low steric hindrance favors backside attack. SN2 dominates unless a sterically hindered strong base (e.g., KOtBu) forces E2.

Strong Nu → SN2
Bulky Base → E2

3. Secondary Substrate (2°, R2CH–LG)

Borderline zone where all four pathways can compete. Look at base strength, nucleophile nature, solvent, and temperature.

Strong Base → E2
Weak Base / Strong Nu → SN2
Weak Nu / Protic → SN1 / E1

4. Tertiary Substrate (3°, R3C–LG)

Backside attack is sterically blocked (SN2 impossible). Strong bases produce E2; weak nucleophiles/bases yield SN1 + E1.

Strong Base → E2
Weak Nu/Base → SN1 (E1 w/ heat)
